# AI/RAG/LLM Tools over European Parliamentary Proceedings (status as of Oct 2026)

> Method note: WebFetch failed with DNS errors (ENOTFOUND) for almost every primary domain tried (gov.uk, cfp.pydata.org, arxiv.org, finestmedia.ee, e-estonia.com, tagesspiegel.de, mirrorweb). Findings below therefore come from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from full-page reads. Treat details as "reported by source X" and re-verify key specifics (model names, numbers) before relying on them. About 22 tool calls were used.

## UK: TheyWorkForYou/mySociety, Hansard AI, UK Parliament search, Parlex (i.AI)

### Takeaway
The most developed UK tool is **Parlex**, built by the government's i.AI unit for civil servants, not for the public. It does semantic and hybrid search over Hansard and gives cited summaries, and it is framed as "anticipating MP/Peer reactions to policy". I found no verified official UK Parliament RAG/chat feature over Hansard, and no published mySociety/TheyWorkForYou LLM product. mySociety lists AI for transparency as a research theme only.

### Cited Findings
- **What Parlex is:** a UK government tool from the Incubator for AI (i.AI) and part of the "Humphrey" suite of civil-service AI tools. It searches and analyses parliamentary records so that policy teams can anticipate how MPs and Peers may react to a proposal. — [civictech.guide](https://civictech.guide/projects/parlex-and-lex); [UKAuthority on Humphrey](https://www.ukauthority.com/articles/dsit-unveils-humphrey-ai-package-for-civil-service)
- **Transparency record:** the DSIT Algorithmic Transparency Record calls Parlex a "parliamentary intelligence tool" for civil servants. It combines semantic search with AI-assisted analysis over Hansard, legislation, caselaw and Parliamentary Questions. It offers daily summaries, and generative AI produces **cited summaries** of search results. The record states that insights **link back to the original parliamentary records**. — [gov.uk ATRS: DSIT - Parlex](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/dsit-parlex)
- **Data and retrieval:** Parlex is built on the existing **Hansard API**. The i.AI case study says the team applied **hybrid search** and other ML techniques. — [ai.gov.uk knowledge hub (UKGWA archive)](https://tnaqa.mirrorweb.com/ukgwa/20260902044454mp_/https://ai.gov.uk/knowledge-hub/tools/parlex)
- **Reported time saving:** in early user testing, researching a parliamentarian took 1–3 minutes with Parlex, against an estimated 30–60 minutes without it. This comes from a very small sample (about 3 interviewees), so it is weak evidence. — same i.AI knowledge hub page
- **Demo example:** the public demo used a 20mph speed-limit policy and showed party-split reactions (Labour MPs supporting traffic calming, Conservative MPs often opposing). — [BOOM Live](https://boomlive.in/web-stories/news/uk-introduces-ai-tool-to-gauge-mps-stance-on-policies-2313)
- **Framing controversy:** January 2025 press coverage described Parlex as an AI "to predict MP response to policy". A Commons written answer on 31 Oct 2025 said the tool was **not trained as a new AI model** and is a search and analysis tool. — [Holyrood](https://holyrood.com/news/view,uk-government-to-use-ai-to-predict-mp-response-to-policy); [Westminster Brief PQ 84244](https://westminsterbrief.co.uk/archive/pq/84244)
- **ParliAI (ONS Data Science Campus):** a government tool that scrapes daily transcripts from all UK chambers through TheyWorkForYou and flags mentions of the ONS. It is an AI-assisted monitoring tool, not a public chat interface. — [ONS Data Science Campus](https://datasciencecampus.ons.gov.uk/parliai-using-ai-tools-to-monitor-parliamentary-coverage-of-the-ons-across-the-uk/)
- **GovernmentGPT:** an independent developer **fine-tuned Mistral 7B** on Commons Hansard from 1997 onward to see whether a model could "debate like MPs". Data starts in 1997 because that period was easiest to link to speaker data. — [Hugging Face: stewhsource/GovernmentGPT](https://huggingface.co/stewhsource/GovernmentGPT)
- **Simulated-debate study:** a 2025 IJPDS study compared debates generated by general LLMs and by GovernmentGPT against real parliamentary debates on ultra-processed foods. — [IJPDS](https://ijpds.org/index.php/ijpds/article/view/3328)
- **ParliaBench (academic):** a benchmark for LLM-generated UK parliamentary speech, built on the UK subset of ParlaMint for 2015–2022. The authors say it measures linguistic quality and "political authenticity" but does **not verify factual accuracy against the record**. — [arXiv 2511.08247](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08247v1); [HF Space demo](https://argyrotsipi-parliabench.hf.space/)
- **mySociety:** the research team lists "application of AI technologies to democratic transparency" as a theme. A 2024 podcast mentions a new and evolving internal AI-use policy. The 2025/26 impact report highlights votes.theyworkforyou.com, a structured voting-data platform, and not an LLM chat feature. — [mySociety democracy](https://www.mysociety.org/democracy/); [podcast ep.1](https://www.mysociety.org/podcast/episode-1-august-2024/); [Impact report 2026](https://research.mysociety.org/html/impact-report-2026/)
- **Polivada (commercial):** a Microsoft Marketplace app for Copilot/Teams that offers AI semantic search of UK parliamentary activity "by concept, not just keyword". — [Microsoft Marketplace](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/en-us/product/WA200010986)

### Inferences
- Parlex shows a pattern worth borrowing: hybrid (lexical plus semantic) search over an official API, summaries that always cite and link to the source record, and per-member profiles. It also shows a risk. Framing a tool as "predicting" politicians' stances drew negative press, and the government had to reframe it as search and analysis. A Knesset project should present itself as search and summarisation and avoid prediction language.
- The ATRS record is a useful template for publishing a transparency or model card for a public-sector parliamentary tool.
- ParliaBench's admitted gap (no factual verification against the record) is a reminder that generation-style evaluation is not enough. A RAG tool needs faithfulness and citation checks.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full ATRS record. The LLM vendor/model behind Parlex, its hallucination testing and its error rates are unconfirmed.
- I found no verified official UK Parliament (parliament.uk / Hansard) generative AI search or chat as of Oct 2026. Ontario's Hansard "Ask a question" AI feature (March 2026) is Canadian and out of scope. — [OLA](https://www.ola.org/en/office-assembly/whats-happening/new-hansard-search-features-now-available)
- I found no evidence that mySociety has shipped an LLM feature on TheyWorkForYou. Its blog was not reachable.
- "Consult" (i.AI) analyses consultation responses, not Hansard. I did not research it in detail here.

## Germany: Bundestag Chat, abgeordnetenwatch, OffenesParlament, Bundestag's own AI

### Takeaway
**Bundestag Chat** is the clearest open-source RAG reference over German plenary speeches. It was presented at PyData Global 2025 with a stack of LlamaIndex, Hugging Face embeddings, PGVector, Chainlit, Langfuse and Ragas. The Bundestag administration is reportedly building an internal privacy-preserving chatbot for MPs. I found no verified LLM features from abgeordnetenwatch or OffenesParlament.

### Cited Findings
- **Bundestag Chat talk:** "Bundestag Chat: Discovering Political Landscape with RAG Systems" by Piotr Kalota and Matthias Boeck, PyData Global 2025, 10 Dec 2025. It presents an **open-source RAG blueprint** for exploring and conversing with roughly a decade of Bundestag speeches. — [PyData Global 2025 talk](https://cfp.pydata.org/pydataglobal2025/talk/YPRZBE/)
- **Stack:** LlamaIndex for parsing and chunking, Hugging Face embeddings, **PGVector** as the vector store, **Chainlit** for the UI, **Langfuse** for observability and tracing, and **Ragas** for evaluation. The talk also covers build-vs-buy trade-offs and scaling. — same source
- **Langfuse–LlamaIndex integration:** a native integration (Feb 2024) captures traces and metrics per request through LlamaIndex's global callback manager. — [Langfuse blog](https://langfuse.com/blog/llama-index-integration)
- **Contextual RAG paper:** a 2025 work on political text corpora (German plenary minutes) uses Contextual RAG because protocols are sequences of speeches. It reports up to **18% better recall@k** than basic RAG. — [Academia.edu: "Einsatz von Contextual-RAG zur Quellenextraktion in politischen Textkorpora", 2025](https://unal.academia.edu/ErnestoCurmen/Books)
- **Internal Bundestag chatbot:** a Bitkom podcast describes an in-house AI chatbot for MPs. It is designed to be privacy-compliant, to flag personal data, and to run online or offline. — [Bitkom Tech Weekly podcast](https://www.bitkom.org/mediathek/podcasts/Tech-Weekly-Wie-digital-ist-der-Bundestag-und-wie-gefaehrlich-ist-Desinformation)
- **Open data:** the Bundestag DIP API provides legislative activity, voting records and debates. — [Substack "Alive and Kicking"](https://developments.substack.com/p/alive-and-kicking)

### Inferences
- The Bundestag Chat stack is directly reusable for a Knesset project. PGVector keeps vectors next to the relational metadata (speaker, party, date, session), which makes speaker and party filters simple. Langfuse adds tracing and Ragas adds offline evaluation (faithfulness, answer relevancy, context precision/recall).
- The Contextual RAG result supports a specific chunking choice. Prepend each chunk with session, agenda item and speaker context instead of chunking raw text, because a protocol is a dialogue of speeches.

### Gaps
- I could not access the talk page, a repository URL, a live demo, or reported Ragas scores for Bundestag Chat. Whether it supports Hebrew-like features (speaker filters, vote linking) is unknown.
- I found no verified AI/LLM features from abgeordnetenwatch.de or OffenesParlament (de/at). Searches returned nothing.
- The status, vendor and launch date of the Bundestag internal chatbot are unconfirmed (podcast source only).

## EU Parliament: Archibot ("Ask the EP Archives"), HowTheyVote.eu, ParlTrack, other LLM tools

### Takeaway
**Archibot / "Ask the EP Archives"** is the flagship official parliamentary RAG deployment in Europe. It launched in October 2024 on Anthropic Claude through Amazon Bedrock, covers more than 2.1M archival documents from 1952 onward, and works across EU languages. It has drawn criticism for insufficient pre-deployment testing. HowTheyVote.eu is non-AI open vote data. Third-party MCP servers over EP open data are emerging.

### Cited Findings
- **Launch:** the European Parliament launched "Ask the EP Archives" (Archibot), built on **Claude via Amazon Bedrock**. It makes more than **2.1 million official documents** available, going back to **1952**. — [Claude customer story](https://claude.com/customers/european-parliament); [Euronews, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/10/22/an-ai-tool-is-making-the-european-parliaments-history-easier-to-understand-in-all-eu-langu)
- **Architecture:** according to EP archives head Ludovic Delépine, it uses **RAG** to combine LLMs with authoritative archival sources. Tasks include retrieving relevant documents, generating concise summaries and producing curated lists of archival materials. — [Electronic Specifier](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=35411); [Business AM (FR)](https://fr.businessam.be/?p=123507)
- **Multilingual access:** the archives were previously mostly in French. The tool lets users query and read them across EU languages. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/10/22/an-ai-tool-is-making-the-european-parliaments-history-easier-to-understand-in-all-eu-langu)
- **Claimed results:** an **80% reduction** in document search and analysis time. This is a vendor-published claim and has not been independently verified. — [Claude customer story](https://claude.com/customers/european-parliament)
- **Criticism:** Kris Shrishak of Enforce (Irish Council for Civil Liberties) criticised the EP for not adequately testing or assessing the Claude deployment before launch. I saw the headline and snippet only. — [Tagesspiegel Background](https://background.tagesspiegel.de/digitalisierung-und-ki/briefing/eu-parlament-forscher-kritisiert-fehlende-pruefung-von-claude-einsatz)
- **Broadcast archive:** Archibot was also presented at the FIAT/IFTA Media Management Seminar 2025. — [FIAT/IFTA](https://fiatifta.org/seminar/media-management-seminar-2025/)
- **HowTheyVote.eu:** a free, open-source site for EP **roll-call votes** with an API and open-licence dataset, presented at FOSDEM 2026. It covers roll-call votes only (no secret or show-of-hands votes). I found no LLM features. — [howtheyvote.eu/about](https://howtheyvote.eu/about); [FOSDEM 2026](https://fosdem.org/schedule/event/39YMYR-how-they-vote); [GitHub](https://github.com/HowTheyVote/howtheyvote)
- **European Parliament MCP Server (hack23):** a third-party MCP server that gives AI assistants access to plenary records and votes through the official EP Open Data API. It includes a `generate_report` tool for "AI-ready summaries". I did not verify it. — [hack23](https://hack23.com/european-parliament-mcp.html)

### Inferences
- Archibot shows that an official parliament can run RAG on a commercial frontier model through a cloud platform for multilingual access. The public criticism over evaluation suggests a Knesset project should publish its evaluation methodology before launch.
- Exposing parliamentary data through MCP (the hack23 pattern) is a cheap way to make a Knesset corpus usable by any LLM client, alongside a dedicated UI.
- Linking votes to speeches is a gap at HowTheyVote, which has votes but no speech RAG. That gap is an opportunity.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Archibot's citation UI, its hallucination safeguards, or any formal evaluation results.
- I found nothing on LLM features in ParlTrack.
- I found no EP-wide official generative AI for plenary verbatim reports (CRE) beyond the archives, apart from the IPU use-case list mentioning "security in the European Parliament".

## France, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Estonia, Nordics

### Takeaway
Outside the UK, Germany and the EU, the most relevant items are the Italian Chamber's 2025 prototypes (NORMA, MSE, DEPUCHAT), the open-source academic **ParliamentRAG** for the Italian Chamber (authority-aware, multi-perspective, evaluated against NotebookLM), and Open State's Dutch experiment. Estonia's "HANS" is speech-to-text transcription, not RAG. France's Assemblée nationale is still assessing AI (June 2026 report). I found no LaBase evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Italy**
- **Chamber prototypes:** on 9 July 2025 the Camera dei Deputati presented three AI prototypes. **NORMA** is a virtual assistant for analysing legislative output that answers natural-language queries about laws and amendments from the Chamber's certified archives. **MSE** is assisted drafting of amendments. **DEPUCHAT** is a chatbot that helps citizens access deputies' activities. They were due to be operational by end 2025. — [AGI, 10 Jul 2025](https://www.agi.it/politica/news/2025-07-10/prototipi-ai-parlamento-depuchat-32257341/); [TuttoTech](https://www.tuttotech.net/news/2025/07/14/norma-mse-depuchat-parlamento-intelligenza-artificiale.html); [Open](https://www.open.online/2025/07/10/lintelligenza-artificiale-parlamento-aiuto-scrittura-leggi/)
- **Budget:** the Chamber planned to spend about half a million euros on AI projects for deputies. The projects came from a February 2024 call led by Vice-President Anna Ascani. — [Pagella Politica](https://pagellapolitica.it/articoli/camera-costi-progetti-intelligenza-artificiale-deputati)
- **ParliamentRAG ("Who Speaks Matters: Authority-Aware Multi-View RAG over Italian Parliamentary Proceedings", arXiv 2608.13410, 2026):** the paper names three failure modes of naive RAG on parliamentary transcripts:
  - **dominance of frequent speakers**
  - inability to weight speakers by topical expertise
  - **citation misattribution** in politically sensitive text

  It uses topic-dependent speaker authority to retrieve expert perspectives and produce faithful **multi-perspective (per-group) summaries**. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13410); [HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13410)
- **ParliamentRAG evaluation:** 15 policy topics, automated metrics, and a **blind A/B human evaluation with 6 domain experts against Google NotebookLM**. It matches NotebookLM on overall satisfaction and does better on **group coverage, quotation faithfulness and source authority**. — same
- **ParliamentRAG code and data:** Apache-2.0 code at github.com/Emeierkeio/thesis-ParliamentRAG, and a Zenodo knowledge graph of the 19th legislature (University of Milano-Bicocca). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13410); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/21602327)
- **Related Italian work:** a paper titled "Towards Building a Trustworthy RAG-Based Chatbot for the Italian Public Administration". — [IOS Press FAIA](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/FAIA250637)

**France**
- **Assemblée nationale report:** Deputies Nicolas Bonnet and Denis Masséglia presented a report on AI use on 17 June 2026. More than 85% of surveyed deputies and staff use AI. ChatGPT dominates (230 daily users), and fewer than 20% use Mistral. The report recommends training on good practice and data protection. — [Assemblée nationale](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/actualites-accueil-hub/les-usages-et-les-besoins-d-intelligence-artificielle-a-l-assemblee-nationale); [LCP](https://lcp.fr/actualites/intelligence-artificielle-comment-des-deputes-veulent-securiser-l-utilisation-de-l-ia)
- **Albert:** a public generative AI developed by DINUM on open-source models (Llama 3.1, Mistral). It is also offered as an API for administrations to build their own products. It is not specific to parliament. — [Presse-citron](https://www.presse-citron.net/albert-3-choses-a-savoir-sur-cette-ia-de-letat-qui-ne-plait-pas-a-tout-le-monde/); [Acteurs publics](https://acteurspublics.fr/articles/albert-une-ia-generative-publique-prometteuse-pour-les-agents-des-france-services)
- **Academic (France):**
  - HistoriQA-ThirdRepublic is a multi-hop QA corpus over French Third Republic debates (1870–1940). It evaluates RAG with an LLM-as-judge validated against human experts, and finds a sharp accuracy drop on multi-hop questions. — [arXiv 2606.31325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31325)
  - An EPITA/LRE poster evaluates automatic source attribution in parliamentary debates. — [LRE EPITA](https://www.lre.epita.fr/posts/2025-07-30-publication-mnshs-poster-evalllm/)

**Netherlands**
- **Open State Foundation:** a BZK-subsidised project (2023 tender) to fine-tune two LLMs on open Tweede Kamer data and have them "debate" each other in a chat interface. The goal is to show generative-AI risks to democracy in a non-technical way, with privacy and impact assessments included. — [Open State tender PDF](https://openstate.eu/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/11/Opdracht-voor-externe-inhuur-LLM-finetunen.pdf); [Eerste Kamer doc](https://eerstekamer.nl/9370000/1/j4nvi0xeni9vr2l_j9vvkyblr2b3ny8/vme7lwyna9yz/f=/vme6krjs8ooc.pdf)
- **BBB party chatbot:** the party built a chatbot over its MP's debate contributions and written questions. It answers with source references, and the party says it was tuned to reduce hallucination. — [Binnenlands Bestuur](https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/digitaal/kunstmatige-intelligentie/bbb-chatbot-smaakt-naar-meer)
- **Bias concern:** an RTL Z test (Oct 2025) found ChatGPT, DeepSeek and Grok leaned toward D66, GroenLinks-PvdA and Volt on Kieskompas statements. Experts warned that AI is not politically neutral. — [Welingelichte Kringen](https://www.welingelichtekringen.nl/tech/op-welke-partij-stemmen-chatgpt-en-andere-ai-chatbots-en-is-het-een-goed-idee-om-de-app-om-hulp-te-vragen)

**Estonia**
- **HANS:** the Riigikogu's speech-recognition verbatim-records system, built by Finestmedia with TalTech ASR and live since September 2020. Editors correct the text before publication. It now covers plenaries and committees, with about a **5% error rate** according to Ahto Saks. It is transcription only. I found no evidence of RAG or Q&A. — [IPU use case](https://www.ipu.org/ai-use-cases/ai-powered-verbatim-records-system-hans); [IPU innovation tracker](https://ipu.org/innovation-tracker/story/view-estonia-ai-advanced-beginners); [e-estonia](https://e-estonia.com/estonian-parliament-uses-speech-recognition-technology-to-create-verbatim-records/); [ERR](https://www.err.ee/1134953/eesti-viimaste-stenografistide-too-vottis-ule-robot)

**Iceland**
- **Althingi:** a two-year project with Reykjavik University on ASR for Althingi speeches (transcription). — [Reykjavik University](https://en.ru.is/news/artificial-intelligence-to-be-used-in-the-recording-of-parliament-speeches)

**IPU (cross-cutting)**
- **Use-case library:** the IPU Centre for Innovation in Parliament, with its Parliamentary Data Science Hub, publishes "Use cases for AI in parliaments" (Dec 2024). Entries include a parliamentary debate assistant that uses transcripts converted into LLM-consumable structures, chatbots over documentation, and session summary assistants. The IPU notes these are use-case templates, not case studies of deployed systems. — [IPU publication](https://www.ipu.org/resources/publications/reference/2024-12/use-cases-ai-in-parliaments); [Debate assistant](https://www.ipu.org/ai-use-cases/parliamentary-debate-assistant); [IPU AI guidelines](https://ipu.org/ai-guidelines/role-ai-in-parliaments)
- **Secondary reports:** a PSA blog post reports that Italy's Chamber of Deputies provides a chatbot for querying proceedings and that Estonia uses AI for live subtitling. These are secondary sources. — [PSA Parliaments](https://psaparliaments.org/2025/01/16/democratic-innovation-through-ai-in-parliaments/)

### Inferences
- ParliamentRAG is the single most transferable design reference for political neutrality. It retrieves per party or group, weights speakers by topical authority instead of frequency, enforces verbatim quotation faithfulness, and evaluates against a strong commercial baseline with blind expert review. A Knesset tool should do the same, especially on coalition/opposition balance.
- Measured or claimed numbers from official deployments are scarce: Archibot's 80% (vendor), Parlex's 1–3 vs 30–60 minutes (n of about 3), HANS's 5% WER. Most official tools lack published RAG evaluations. Publishing one is a differentiator.
- Dutch evidence of LLM partisan lean argues for neutrality testing, for example symmetric prompts per party and checks of party coverage balance in answers.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence for "LaBase" at the Assemblée nationale. It may not exist under that name.
- I found no Spanish (Congreso), Finnish (Eduskunta), Danish (Folketing), Swedish or Norwegian parliamentary RAG or chat tools. Searches in English returned only ASR and vendor pages. Native-language searches were not completed.
- The DEPUCHAT and NORMA architecture, LLM vendor, current status (late 2026) and evaluation are unknown.
- The current status and results of Open State's fine-tuned debating models are unknown, since only the 2023 tender was found.

## Lessons worth borrowing for a Hebrew Knesset RAG project (synthesis across sources)

### Takeaway
Use a hybrid-search RAG with chunks that carry speaker and session context. Store everything in Postgres/PGVector with rich metadata filters. Require verbatim, linked citations. Retrieve in a way that balances parties and groups. Add tracing and offline evaluation (Langfuse and Ragas, or equivalents) plus blind expert review against a strong baseline. Publish a transparency record, and avoid "prediction" framing.

### Cited Findings
- Hybrid search over the official API, with summaries cited and linked back to records (Parlex). — [gov.uk ATRS](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/dsit-parlex)
- The LlamaIndex + PGVector + Chainlit + Langfuse + Ragas open blueprint (Bundestag Chat). — [PyData](https://cfp.pydata.org/pydataglobal2025/talk/YPRZBE/)
- Contextual chunking for speech-sequence transcripts, with up to +18% recall@k. — [Academia.edu](https://unal.academia.edu/ErnestoCurmen/Books)
- Authority-aware, per-group retrieval, quotation faithfulness, and blind expert A/B testing against NotebookLM (ParliamentRAG). — [arXiv 2608.13410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13410)
- An LLM-as-judge validated against human experts, plus multi-hop QA test sets (HistoriQA). — [arXiv 2606.31325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31325)
- A commercial LLM over cloud infrastructure for multilingual archive access, which drew criticism over pre-launch testing (Archibot). — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/10/22/an-ai-tool-is-making-the-european-parliaments-history-easier-to-understand-in-all-eu-langu); [Tagesspiegel](https://background.tagesspiegel.de/digitalisierung-und-ki/briefing/eu-parlament-forscher-kritisiert-fehlende-pruefung-von-claude-einsatz)
- Reputational risk from framing a tool as "predicting MPs" (Parlex press coverage and the government's reframing). — [Holyrood](https://holyrood.com/news/view,uk-government-to-use-ai-to-predict-mp-response-to-policy); [PQ answer](https://westminsterbrief.co.uk/archive/pq/84244)

### Inferences
- **Recommended evaluation set for the Knesset:**
  - a Ragas-style suite (faithfulness, context precision/recall)
  - a citation-correctness check (is the quoted speaker the actual speaker?), which addresses ParliamentRAG's misattribution failure mode
  - party-coverage balance metrics
  - multi-hop questions
  - a blind expert A/B test against NotebookLM or a frontier chat model given the same documents
- **Features seen across tools worth including:** speaker, party and date filters; per-member profiles (Parlex); linking speeches to votes, which no surveyed tool clearly does and which is an opportunity; daily digests (Parlex); and an MCP or API endpoint (hack23).

### Gaps
- None of the European tools found clearly offers **video-timestamp linking** from answers to debate video. I could not verify this for Archibot or Bundestag Chat.
- I found no European tool handling a right-to-left or Semitic language. Hebrew-specific lessons (morphology, embeddings) must come from other research.
